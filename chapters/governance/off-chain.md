# Off-Chain Governance

Off-chain governance reaches decisions through discussion, review, rough consensus, releases, and voluntary adoption rather than a contract that counts votes and executes the result. Bitcoin and Ethereum use this model for core protocol changes.

## How decisions become real

Mailing lists, forums, calls, research papers, issue trackers, and conferences expose proposals and objections. Editors publish specifications. Developers implement changes. Maintainers release software. Operators and users decide whether to adopt it.

There may be no final ballot. Rough consensus means objections have been considered and the remaining support is sufficient for implementation or coordination. The meaning depends on the process. BIP 3 defines criteria for Process BIPs; Ethereum's EIP process describes rough consensus among client implementers for Core EIP coordination.

## Strengths and weaknesses

Discussion can weigh technical arguments without reducing every participant to token balance. It can adapt to unusual situations and preserve the option to refuse software. It is also hard to measure, time-consuming, and influenced by who has expertise, time, language access, funding, and control of communication venues.

No formal vote means claims of “community consensus” need evidence. Cite meetings, implementations, public commitments, releases, and adoption rather than treating silence as agreement.

## Social recovery

Severe failures can push communities outside normal procedures. Participants may coordinate a client patch, rollback, emergency fork, or rejection of an attacking chain. This capacity is a last-resort social layer. Using it changes expectations about finality and neutrality, so legitimacy and scope matter as much as technical feasibility.

## Combining on-chain and off-chain processes

DAOs often discuss and draft off-chain, signal through an off-chain poll, then execute on-chain. Protocols may use a security council for emergencies and token voting for routine changes. Hybrid governance should state which stage is binding and who can override it.
